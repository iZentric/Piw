    DESCRIPTION

    The DT-75 tractor has been manufactured at the Volgograd Tractor Plant (VgTZ) since 1963, with some sources suggesting production started as early as 1962. Large-scale manufacturing at the Pavlodar Tractor Plant (PTZ) took place between 1968 and 1992, with limited production continuing into the 1990s. Between 1962 and 1995, VgTZ produced approximately 1,652,500 units, while PTZ manufactured around 862,300 units from 1968 to 1995.


    The first two prototype DT-75 tractors were assembled at VgTZ on July 16, 1956. In May 1960, a decision was made to modernize the plant and transition to full-scale DT-75 production. Mass production of the DT-75M variant began at VgTZ on August 28, 1967. The DT-75B swamp tractor followed in 1969, though some sources indicate it may have been introduced in 1968. PTZ rolled out its first tractor, the DT-75M, on August 12, 1968.


    One key distinction between the tractors produced at the two plants is branding—PTZ models featured "Kazakhstan" on the hood instead of "DT-75." The color scheme also varied: PTZ models included blue with a matte white cab top (DT-75M) and yellow for models like DT-75ML and DT-75T, whereas VgTZ tractors were typically red. Additionally, certain models with a left-side fuel tank, such as the DT-75V, DT-75MV, and DT-75N, were exclusive to VgTZ. Meanwhile, PTZ was the sole producer of yellow tractors with large cabs, such as the DT-75ML and DT-75T.
